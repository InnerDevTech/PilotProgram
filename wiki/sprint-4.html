Expected outcome

By the end of Sprint 4 everything built so far should be working together in one loop. The projector draws balls of light; the camera supplies real object positions from Sprint 3; the IR frame supplies hand positions from Sprint 2. Balls bounce off the desk edges, off real objects as you slide them (with the object flashing on contact), and off hands.

A fast hand swipe should push a ball; a hand held still over one for about 0.6 seconds should pick it up. The constants at the top of web/physics.js — bounce, friction, hold time — get tuned by playtesting, with every change written down so you can explain the choices later, and any case that broke during tuning becomes a new test.

The sprint ends with a 3-minute phone recording of the whole desk running, which is also your backup if the live demo misbehaves in Sprint 6.

Demo checklist

  • Balls bounce off all walls.
  • A slid object pushes a ball, and the object flashes when hit.
  • A fast hand swipe pushes a ball.
  • A hovering hand picks up a ball.
  • The screen never shows a ball off the edge of the projection.

Definition of done

A 2-minute run with no crash, no stuck balls, and no balls leaving the screen.

Risk to watch: the browser pauses animation when the window is hidden. Keep the desk page in front and full screen during demos.
