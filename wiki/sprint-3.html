Status
Not started
Changed from the plan. Sprint 2 switched the hand sensor from IR beams to the webcam, and a camera needs calibrating before it can place anything. So the calibration this sprint was going to build — turning camera pixels into desk positions, saving calibration.json, the bridge’s --camera option — was built in Sprint 2 instead (bridge/calibrate_desk.py and bridge/desk_geometry.py). The plan’s 24-dot calibrate.py is not needed. What is left of calibration here is proving it is accurate. That frees up this sprint for the objects, and for mounting the camera properly.

User stories

  • As a user, I can name my objects in objects.json and see their names on the desk.
  • As a user, calibration checks itself and tells me if it is inaccurate.
  • As a user, my hand and my objects are tracked at the same time by the one camera.

Already done in Sprint 2

  • Camera pixels to desk coordinates (the homography), saved in calibration.json.
  • Four-corner calibration tool with a grid drawn back over the camera picture.
  • Bridge option to turn the camera on (--desk, or --camera with a name or number).
  • A page layer over the screens that can draw on the desk (the hand dot uses it; the rings will too).

Expected outcome

By the end of Sprint 3 the camera is fixed above the desk looking straight down, and the calibration from Sprint 2 has been re-done for that position and measured at under 2% error — checked, not taken on trust.

A printed marker on a real object makes a ring appear around it on the desk: blue with its name if it is listed in objects.json, amber if it is not. The ring stays on the object as it is slid around, within about 2 cm of its centre, and the hand dot keeps working at the same time from the same camera.

This is the last sprint of the MVP. If Sprints 1 to 3 pass, the project is a success even if Sprint 4 never happens.

Demo checklist

  • The camera is on its permanent mount and the bridge reports the hand sensor connected.
  • Average calibration error is under 2%.
  • A registered object shows its name and a blue ring.
  • Moving the object moves the ring with it.
  • An unregistered object shows an amber ring.
  • The hand dot still works with objects on the desk.

Definition of done

The ring stays on the object for 2 minutes of moving it around.

Risk to watch: the projector or camera gets bumped. Mark their positions with tape, and re-run calibrate_desk.py after any move. Second risk: a hand over a marker hides it from the camera — the ring should hold for a moment instead of vanishing, the same way the hand dot rides out a dropped frame.
