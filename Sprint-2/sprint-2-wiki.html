Expected outcome

By the end of Sprint 2 a grid of 16 IR beams — 8 across X, 8 across Y — should be mounted in a fixed frame around the desk and wired to the Arduino Mega. Blocking any beam flips its digit in the BEAMS line, and the crossing point of the blocked X and Y beams becomes a hand position that the desk page draws as a glowing dot.

The dot should track a real hand to within about one beam spacing (6 cm) and should be there roughly nine times out of ten. Just as important, it should not appear when the desk is empty: the thresholds BLOCKED_BELOW and CLEAR_ABOVE need tuning so room light does not read as a hand, and pressing C should re-baseline the sensor when the lighting changes.

No camera and no physics yet — the dot is the whole deliverable.

Demo checklist

  • All 16 beams report a 1 when blocked and 0 when clear.
  • Hand dot follows a hand from left to right and top to bottom.
  • Pressing C recalibrates, and the dot stops moving with an empty desk.
  • The Serial Monitor shows no WARNING lines.

Definition of done

The hand dot works for 5 minutes with no false hands, on the real desk.

Risk to watch: strong sunlight or a desk lamp shining on the receivers. Plan a shade or run the demo in a dim room, and note it in the team log.
